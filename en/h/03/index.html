Room 03. Who is watching? Who is watching?Who is watching?Who is watching?

Кто смотрит?

Hiding a message in plain sight is the oldest art and the oldest technology. Without the key a cipher looks like noise; with it, like text.

Pairs in this room

  1. 03/01 Kryptos ↔ encryption

    Jim Sanborn, 1990

    Everyone sees the Kryptos cipher; no one has read its fourth part. End-to-end encryption works the same way: the server sees only symbols. Turn it on in your messenger.

  2. 03/02 Skull ↔ steganography

    Hans Holbein the Younger, 1533

    Holbein's skull is visible from one spot only. Steganography hides a message the same way, in the lowest bits of a photo. Find the spot on the floor where the word appears.

  3. 03/03 NINA ↔ watermark

    Al Hirschfeld; Dmitri Shostakovich, since 1945; 1960

    Hirschfeld hid the name NINA in his drawings, Shostakovich his initials in notes. Files are signed the same way, with an invisible watermark. Play D–E♭–C–B on the keyboard.

  4. 03/04 Caesar ↔ E2E

    A history of ciphers, 1st century BC to today

    Two thousand years of ciphers, from Caesar's shift to a key that lives only in your phone. Without the key it is noise, with it — text. Decrypt today's word with your passport key.

  5. 03/05 Cable ↔ metadata

    Trevor Paglen; Evan Roth; Simon Weckert, 2015; since 2016; 2020

    The internet is physical: cables, shores, 99 phones in a cart that fooled a map. Your phone keeps broadcasting too. Remove the location tag from a photo before posting it.
