The idea

Trust, but verify.

A museum and a security team do the same job. A conservator X-rays a painting to see what lies under the paint. A cryptographer checks a signature to learn who really sent the message. And each of us decides ten times a day whether this email, this call, this face on the screen is real.

Artists learned to hide, to forge and to expose long before computers. Holbein hid a skull in a state portrait, van Meegeren sold museums a “Vermeer” he had painted himself, and a sculpture in the courtyard of the CIA has kept an unsolved cipher for thirty-five years. In this exhibition every artwork stands next to its digital twin: the trick the artist used, and the same trick in code.

Four rooms ask the four questions every security system asks: Who are you? Is it real? Who is watching? What if it breaks? Take a passport at the entrance. Each room holds one check. Pass all four and you leave with a certificate of authenticity — your own.

Cyber means helmsman

Greek κυβερνήτης is the helmsman, the one who steers the ship. In 1948 Norbert Wiener named the science of control and communication “Cybernetics”. Cybersecurity grew out of that word.

physicstoday.aip.org

A proverb the world knows in Russian

Signing the INF Treaty on 8 December 1987, Ronald Reagan said it in Russian: “Doveryai, no proveryai.” Mikhail Gorbachev replied: “You repeat that at every meeting.” In 2010 Forrester analyst John Kindervag turned the same idea into the Zero Trust security model: trust no one by default, verify every request.

en.wikipedia.org, reaganlibrary.gov, isc2.org

The verification passport

Take a passport at the entrance. Each room holds one check: solve it, write the answer and take a stamp.

The answers change every day and are checked only at the bureau. They are not on this site and never will be.

  1. Check 1 Room 01. Who are you?

    Find today's password in the password book (it is on the sign next to it). Write the page number.

    A popular password is a public password.

  2. Check 2 Room 02. Is it real?

    Compare the hash on today's card with the hashes under pictures A–D. Write the letter of the original.

    The eye sees no difference; the hash does. One dot changes everything.

  3. Check 3 Room 03. Who is watching?

    Decrypt today's word on panel 03/04 with the Vigenère table. Key: КОРМЧИЙ.

    Without the key a cipher is noise; with it, text.

  4. Check 4 Room 04. What if it breaks?

    Take a portrait in the photo booth and copy the word from the bottom line of the receipt.

    Data does not have to be kept, and that is fine.

Verification Bureau

Four stamps? Come to the desk. We check your answers and issue a certificate of authenticity in your name. We do not keep your name.

How to get the certificate

  1. Show your passport with the stamps.
  2. Say the name for the certificate.
  3. Take the certificate. The name is erased right after printing.

A card about you

Draw one number about yourself on a card: how many passwords you have, how often you pick up your phone. Pin it to the wall. By the end of the show it becomes a shared work.

Signal

Seven letters hide under red noise along the route. Look at the noise through the red window of your passport and collect the word.

The passport cover has a red window cut into it. It is a light filter: it lets through red light only.

The noise is printed in red ink, the letter beneath it in cyan. Through the filter red merges with the background and cyan turns dark. The noise disappears, the letter stays.

Collect the letters in the order of the route and write the word in your passport. Which word? You will find out only in the rooms.

Signal A bonus, not a condition: you get the certificate without it.

A glitch that works

Every QR code in this exhibition is damaged, up to a quarter of its modules. It still works: the code carries redundancy and can lose 30 %. That is how resilience works, in QR codes and on the internet. The QR codes on the labels lead to the pages of this site, one for each pair.

We keep nothing

  • No attacks on visitors' devices or the museum network; demos run only on our own laptops.
  • The interactives store nothing: no passwords, no faces, no names.
  • The passwords in the book come from the public RockYou training list, without logins or addresses.
  • This site too: no cookies, no counters or analytics, no third-party servers. The fonts are served alongside the pages.

Three things to do tonight

  • Turn on two-factor authentication for your email and messenger.
  • Check the sender's address before you click a link.
  • Update your system and apps tonight.
